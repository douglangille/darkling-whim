I use AI in my writing process. This is how it works as of November 2026.

## How I work

I dictate first. While I might use AI on either end of the dictation process, it does seem to be the best way to get my own voice in play.

I use AI in the ideation and the planning and organizing, for sure. That's a conversation and an iterative process. And I definitely use AI on the other end, to help with the copy and line editing part of the job, to make sure that craft is taken care of and that I'm putting out good work.

But as for the messy, lumpy human prose, that stays mine. I have AI on both sides of this, of course, like any author or writer uses tools, but I keep my words my words.

As always, learning how to incorporate AI in my process is an ongoing experiment. We are all learning here.

## What AI Does

- **Ideation, planning and organizing**: story bibles, beats, structure, in conversation, and iterated
- **Copy and line editing**: so the craft is taken care of
- **Generates options** for concepts, conflicts, or solutions I evaluate and often reject
- **Identifies patterns** (pacing issues, emotional flat spots, continuity problems) that I assess and decide whether to fix

## What I Do

- **Create the vision**: premise, POV, tone, genre rules, story constraints
- **Dictate the prose**: the messy, lumpy human part is mine
- **Make all creative decisions**: what works, what doesn't, what direction we're heading
- **Take responsibility**: Every story published here is under my name. I own it completely.

## The Craft Foundation

I've spent decades practicing and publishing my craft without AI. That foundation -- understanding story structure, recognizing what works and what doesn't, the muscle memory of revision -- is what allows me to strategically use AI tools and maintain human authorship. Not make slop.

AI doesn't give me taste or judgment. It gives me velocity. The taste and judgment are mine, built over years of work.

## The Work You Read

The choices about what to show and what to leave unsaid are mine, and so is the prose as I write it now.

How I think about authorship when AI is involved is in [The Storyteller's Dilemma](https://digital.douglangille.ca/the-storytellers-dilemma/).

## The Bottom Line

I'm not using AI to avoid the hard parts of writing. I'm using it to *reach* the hard parts without losing momentum.

You're reading the result. Judge accordingly.
